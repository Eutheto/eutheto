# eutheto trademark policy

## Scope and status

This policy covers the name **eutheto**, the stylized `eutheto` name, and any logo or other source-identifying artwork later adopted by the project (together, the “Project Marks”). It does not claim that a logo has already been adopted, that any mark is registered, or that a registration has been filed or granted.

The project's legal owner or trademark steward, canonical hosting organization, permission contact, final visual identity, and registration strategy are unresolved governance and identity gates in the [roadmap index](docs/roadmap/README.md) and [active Phase 00 document](docs/roadmap/00-repository-and-reproducible-tooling.md). Rights, if any, remain with their lawful owner; this repository does not invent or transfer ownership.

- **Owner:** the Phase 00 identity and governance decisions required by the roadmap.
- **Closure criterion:** approved records identify the lawful steward and permission contact, establish any official mark and brand assets, document applicable registrations without overstatement, and update this policy and repository notices consistently.

Until that gate closes, there is no project channel able to grant special trademark permission. Do not treat silence, a contribution acceptance, or software-license compliance as permission.

## Relationship to the software and documentation licenses

The Apache License 2.0 permits use, modification, and distribution of covered material under its terms. It does not grant permission to use trade names, trademarks, service marks, or product names except as required for reasonable and customary use in describing the origin of the work and reproducing required notices. Copyright permission and trademark permission are separate.

Nothing in this policy restricts truthful nominative use, commentary, criticism, compatibility statements, or other uses allowed by applicable law. Nothing here is legal advice or expands rights beyond what the lawful owner could grant.

## Uses that generally do not require separate permission

Subject to applicable law and provided the use is truthful and not misleading, you may:

- refer to the unmodified project as `eutheto`;
- state that your work uses, works with, is derived from, or is based on `eutheto`;
- link to the canonical project once the hosting-organization gate closes;
- reproduce the name where necessary to preserve license, copyright, attribution, provenance, or notice text;
- discuss, review, teach, criticize, or report news about the project;
- identify an unmodified copy using the same name when the surrounding presentation makes its source and unofficial distributor clear.

Use plain text where practical. Make the relationship explicit with wording such as “unofficial,” “community-maintained,” “derived from,” or “compatible with.” Do not imply sponsorship, endorsement, certification, release status, or security support.

## Modified versions, forks, and distributions

You may describe a modified work as a fork of or derived from `eutheto`, but the modified product, executable, package, service, installer, repository, domain, social account, or organization must have a clearly distinct primary name and presentation. The `eutheto` name must not be the dominant product name for a modified distribution.

Do not use a Project Mark, confusingly similar spelling, or a look-alike logo in a way likely to make people believe an unofficial or modified work is an official build. Prominent notices must say that the work is modified, identify the responsible distributor, and avoid claims that project maintainers built, audited, signed, support, or endorse it.

Working identifiers in the roadmap are not public brands. In particular, the working CLI name `optimizer` and working file extension `.optplan` are unresolved examples and must not be presented, registered, or promoted as final `eutheto` identifiers. Reverse-domain application IDs, stable/beta channel names, signing identities, and installer associations remain gated as well.

## Logos and visual identity

No official logo permission is available until the project adopts exact brand assets and records their steward and license. Do not create or circulate artwork as the “official eutheto logo,” extract branding from development mockups, or use a look-alike logo to imply an official relationship.

After official assets exist, merely changing colors, proportions, text, or composition does not make a confusing use independent. Accessibility accommodations and technically necessary resizing should preserve source attribution and must not imply endorsement.

## Uses requiring permission

Unless applicable law independently permits the use, obtain written permission from the recorded trademark steward before using a Project Mark:

- in the primary name, logo, domain, application ID, package scope, social handle, or branding of a modified product, hosted service, organization, event, certification, training program, or publication series;
- on merchandise, promotional goods, fundraising material, or paid advertising;
- in a way that suggests official status, sponsorship, endorsement, partnership, certification, security review, or release support;
- as part of another trademark or an application to register a trademark, company name, domain, or source-identifying logo;
- for an unmodified official-style binary or installer when its provenance, contents, signatures, or update channel differ from an official release.

Because the permission contact is not yet established, these uses must wait for the gate to close. No contributor or informal maintainer can grant permission on behalf of an unidentified owner.

## Accurate compatibility and attribution wording

A compatibility statement should be factual, appropriately prominent, and no broader than tested evidence. Prefer:

> Example Tool is an independent project designed to work with eutheto. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the eutheto project.

Do not use terms such as “official,” “certified,” “approved,” “verified by eutheto,” or “eutheto edition” without written authority and evidence for that exact claim. Do not copy the project's overall visual presentation so closely that a reasonable user could mistake source, update channel, support, or security responsibility.

## Questions, permission, and concerns

The project currently publishes no trademark email address, organization, or private permission channel. Do not send legal or personal information to guessed addresses or individual contributors, and do not open a public thread containing confidential legal material. Once the ownership and contact gate closes, this section will identify the canonical channel and the information needed for a permission request or concern.

A future steward should consider the nature of the use, clarity about source, quality and security implications, compatibility evidence, public-interest value, geographic scope, duration, and the risk of confusion. Permission may be limited or conditioned and must be recorded in writing. The absence of enforcement in one instance does not grant permission in another.
